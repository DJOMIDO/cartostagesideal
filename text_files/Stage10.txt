        Overview

Large Language Models (LLMs) are widely used for tasks such as assisting with daily activities, engaging in discussions, and supporting reasoning. Their potential applications are vast. However, like many advanced systems, LLMs often function as "black boxes," making it difficult to discern how their outputs are generated.
Ensuring transparency and explainability in these models is essential for enhancing user comprehension and enabling more accurate and responsible usage. This aligns with the core objectives of Explainable
AI (XAI).

A unique feature of LLMs is their ability to be interrogated and explored through direct questioning. Unlike traditional black-box systems, LLMs can provide self-explanatory insights. Users can query the model about its reasoning, enabling a reflective dialogue. This process is reminiscent of psychological analysis, where reasoning and underlying thoughts are explored through conversation.


        Importance of Sentiment Understanding

Sentiment understanding is critical in fostering collaboration and mutual understanding. Sentiments influence how messages are perceived, intent is interpreted, and responses are formulated. By analyzing how
LLMs process sentiment, we can gain insights into their capacity for nuanced communication and improve their utility in collaborative and interactive environments.


        Objectives

This internship will focus on investigating the intrinsic mechanisms of
LLMs with respect to sentiment analysis. The primary objective is not to analyze sentiment in existing texts but to map how LLMs internally represent and process sentiment-related concepts. This will be achieved through:

 1. Direct Questioning:

-   Probing LLMs to extract insights about their knowledge and
    reasoning concerning emotions.

-   Analyzing responses to identify patterns and strategies used by
    the model to express sentiment-related reasoning.

 2. Internal Representation Analysis:

-   Exploring the internal embeddings of LLMs to trace how
    sentiment concepts are encoded.

-   Utilizing tokenization techniques to extract sentiment-related
    tokens.

-   Measuring distances between sentiment-related tokens in the
    embedding space to understand how sentiment is structured
    within the model.

 3. Visualization:

-   Representing extracted data visually to illustrate the LLM's
    semantic understanding of sentiment.

-   Applying dimensionality reduction techniques (e.g., t-SNE, PCA)
    and visual simplification methods (e.g., Edge Bundling) to
    create interpretable visualizations of sentiment representations.

 4. Model Comparison:

-   Comparing sentiment representations across different LLMs,
    including small, large, and quantized models.

-   Highlighting differences and nuances in how these models encode
    and process sentiment through visual evidence.
